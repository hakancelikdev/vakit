What sets it apart

• Times are computed on the device — the Turkish Diyanet method is implemented as its own calculator and measured against the authority's published calendar. • The sky is part of the app: the sun and the moon are placed astronomically and the palette moves through seven stages, from first light to deep night; the same palette continues in the widgets, on the Lock Screen and on Apple Watch. • Worship records stay with the person: make-up prayers and fasts, hatim progress, dhikr and goals live on the device and in the user's own iCloud. • Advertising is the norm in this category; Vakit shows none at all. The app's only source of income is optional donations from users.

Feature highlights

• Prayer times, a Qibla compass, karahat and nafl windows, and a suhoor alarm that sounds through silent mode and Focus • Imsakiye: a whole month of prayer times in one table, the way printed calendars show them • The Qur'an: two mushaf scripts, word-by-word translation, eight reciters, your own playlists, offline download • 36,000+ hadith across seven collections, the 99 Names, a dhikr counter and the post-prayer tasbihat • Ledgers for missed prayers and fasts, worship goals, hatim tracking and an accounting screen • Step-by-step guides for Fridays and the holy days, plus Diyanet's weekly sermon • 25 widgets, Dynamic Island and Live Activity, six Control Center controls, 13 Apple Watch complications, a Mac menu bar item

Developer

Vakit is written solo by Hakan Çelik — no funding, no subscription. The goal is a calmer daily practice: the right time, a fast launch, an interface that does not shout, and data that is never sold.
